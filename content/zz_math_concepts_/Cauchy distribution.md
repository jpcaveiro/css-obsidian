
The Cauchy distribution is often used in statistics as the canonical example of a "[pathological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathological_\(mathematics\) "Pathological (mathematics)")" distribution since both its [expected value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expected_value "Expected value") and its [variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variance "Variance") are undefined.

The $Cauchy(x_0,\gamma)$ density
$$\frac{1}{\pi\gamma\,\left[1 + \left(\frac{x-x_0}{\gamma}\right)^2\right]}$$
The special case when $x_{0}=0$ and $\gamma =1$ is called the **standard Cauchy distribution** with the probability density function:
$$f(x;x_0=0,\gamma=1)={\frac {1}{\pi \left(1+x^{2}\right)}}.$$
