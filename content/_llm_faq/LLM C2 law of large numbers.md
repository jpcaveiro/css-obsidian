The **Law of Large Numbers (LLN)** is a **theorem** (actually a family of theorems) in probability.

In simple terms:

- If you repeat the same random experiment many times, and
- take the average of the results,

then that average gets closer and closer to the true expected value (the population mean).

Example: for a fair coin, if you code heads = 1 and tails = 0, the expected value is $0.5$. As the number of tosses grows, the sample average tends to $0.5$.

There are two common versions:

1. **Weak Law of Large Numbers**: convergence in probability.  
2. **Strong Law of Large Numbers**: convergence almost surely (a stronger statement).

It is a theorem, and one of the foundational results connecting probability theory with observed long-run frequencies.
